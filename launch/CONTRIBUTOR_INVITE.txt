PURPOSE: Copy-and-paste invitations for individual people. Six variants, each plain UTF-8 with Unix LF line endings.

HOW TO USE: Pick one variant, copy the text under it, replace every <PLACEHOLDER>, and delete the USE WHEN and CHANNEL lines before sending. Send these to people individually. The broadcast announcements are the six posts in this directory; these are the messages that actually recruit somebody, and they only work because they are addressed to a person and reference something that person did.

BEFORE SENDING: every link points at github.com/mojoboost-ml, which is live only after the transfer described in ORG_MIGRATION_CHECKLIST.txt. If the transfer has not happened, either wait or substitute github.com/mojoboost-ml/mojoboost.

WHAT NOT TO DO: do not send more than one of these to the same person, do not send a cold invite to somebody who has never expressed interest in Mojo or in gradient boosting, and do not describe mojoboost as anything other than an experimental alpha. The project's whole credibility rests on not overstating, and a recruiting message is the easiest place to lose that.

VARIANT 2: COLD INVITE, GRADIENT BOOSTING BACKGROUND
USE WHEN: reaching someone who works on GBDT algorithms, LightGBM or XGBoost internals, or tabular machine learning.
CHANNEL: email or direct message.

Hi <NAME>,

I am building mojoboost, a gradient-boosted decision tree library written from scratch in Mojo, and I am looking for people who know what LightGBM actually does rather than what its documentation says it does.

The shape is familiar: quantile binning into uint8, histogram accumulation with the sibling subtraction trick, leaf-wise growth, the standard second-order gain. What is different is that the hot loop is written in a language with explicit SIMD and compile-time specialization, and the same source targets the GPU. The bet is a substantially simpler codebase at competitive speed. That bet is not settled yet.

Where I could use somebody with your background is differential testing against LightGBM, and the parity contract in docs/LIGHTGBM_PARITY.md, which is the file that states what is supported and which differences are deliberate. Every gap you find in it is a real gap. Categorical handling, ranking objectives, and missing-value semantics are the areas where I would least trust my own reading of LightGBM's behavior.

Repository: https://github.com/mojoboost-ml/mojoboost
Parity contract: https://github.com/mojoboost-ml/mojoboost/blob/main/docs/LIGHTGBM_PARITY.md

It is an experimental alpha and I would rather hear that something is wrong than that it looks nice. Filing an issue that says "LightGBM does X here and this does Y" is a complete contribution and needs no code.

VARIANT 3: HARDWARE VALIDATION ASK
USE WHEN: reaching someone who has NVIDIA or AMD hardware, or a Mac of a generation nobody has tested. This is the highest-value ask in the file, because it is specific, bounded, and something the maintainers cannot do themselves.
CHANNEL: anywhere. Also works as a comment on a thread where somebody mentioned their hardware.

Hi <NAME>,

You mentioned you have <SPECIFIC HARDWARE>. Would you be willing to run one validation pass on it?

mojoboost is a gradient-boosted tree library written in Mojo, with a GPU path. Its correctness has been exercised on an Apple M4 and on nothing else. NVIDIA and AMD have never been validated on real hardware, and the repository says so rather than implying otherwise. A single run from a machine I do not own is worth more to this project right now than almost anything I could write myself.

The protocol is https://github.com/mojoboost-ml/mojoboost/blob/main/docs/HARDWARE_CONTRIBUTORS.md and it was written for exactly this, meaning somebody who has the hardware and no interest in learning Mojo. There is a capture script for your vendor under hardware/capture/, a JSON record template it fills in, and an issue form that takes the result. A clone, a pixi install, one script. Perhaps an hour, most of it waiting.

A failure is a better result than a success and I would rather have it. If it does not build, does not run, or produces the wrong numbers, that is the finding, and pasting the unedited output into an issue is the whole contribution. Worth saying because it trips people up: a GPU suite that reports "skipped, no accelerator" on a machine that has an accelerator is not a pass, it is one of the more interesting failures you could send me.

VARIANT 6: OFFERING WRITE ACCESS
USE WHEN: an active contributor has cleared the bar in GOVERNANCE.md and has not asked. Do not wait to be asked; most people will not.
CHANNEL: GitHub comment on their most recent merged pull request, or direct message.

Concretely that means being added to the contributors team, which carries Write on the repository. You push to main directly for anything small and self-contained, you open a pull request when you want another pair of eyes or when the change is big enough that others should see it coming, and the only hard rules are running the focused tests before you push, never force-pushing main, and not self-merging a change to workflows, dependencies, or packaging. GOVERNANCE.md has the full list.

What it does not mean: any obligation to keep contributing, to review anybody else's work, or to be available. Going quiet is not doing anything wrong, and the access does not come with expectations attached.
