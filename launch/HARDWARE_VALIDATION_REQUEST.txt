--- LONG VERSION: GitHub Discussions, the Modular forum, and mailing lists ---

MojoBoost is a gradient-boosted decision tree library written natively in Mojo, with a LightGBM-familiar Python API. It has one GPU source spanning every backend it targets: the histogram and partition kernels, the device-resident tree growth, and a tiling policy that derives launch geometry from device attributes at runtime. There is no CUDA file, no HIP file, no Metal file, and no vendor branch anywhere in it.

That is a design commitment, and it is worth exactly as much as the evidence that the one source is correct on every backend it claims to target. Here is the evidence. One Apple M4, correctness and determinism passing, phase timings printing but not recorded as a sweep, no profiler trace. That is the entire body of hardware evidence this project has. No NVIDIA device and no AMD device has ever executed this code, on a laptop or in CI, and no Apple chip other than that M4 has either. The status tables in the repository say "not run" in most cells because most cells have not been run, and they will keep saying it until somebody with the hardware runs them.

What a first record settles, per device. On any NVIDIA board: whether the shared source builds, computes the right answer, and repeats bit-identically on CUDA. Determinism is the interesting one, because fixed-point Int32 accumulation is what buys it and a backend whose atomics or scheduling differ from Metal's is exactly where it would break. On any AMD board: the same three questions on HIP, and which of consumer RDNA and datacenter CDNA you ran, since a result from one says nothing about the other. On an M1, M2, M3, or M5: whether the tiling policy's fallbacks are right on a chip that answers a different set of device attributes, because Metal refuses six of eleven attribute queries on the recorded M4 and nobody knows what the others refuse.

What contributing involves. Clone, `pixi install`, run one read-only capture script that installs nothing and uploads nothing, run the test suites, fill in a JSON template, attach the raw terminal output, open an issue. Around thirty minutes for a correctness-only record, which is a complete contribution and the bar for a first record on any device. Two to three hours for a full record with a phase-timing sweep. Half a day if you also produce a profiler trace, which is rare and is the only thing that could ever justify a device-specific change to the code.

The rules that make a record worth having, in case they tell you whether this is your kind of project. A GPU suite that prints "skipped: no accelerator" is not a pass, and on a machine with a GPU it means the build did not see the device. A throughput number without a loss number next to it is not a result. Raw output is required, because a record without it is a claim rather than evidence. A failure, a refused card, or a Mac with no Metal compiler is a complete and publishable result, and there is a status for it. Your record is about your device and will never be generalized into a statement about a vendor or an architecture.

Everything is Apache 2.0, attribution is automatic and is the only thing on offer, and a withdrawal request is honored.
