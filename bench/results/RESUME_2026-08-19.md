## THE DIAGNOSIS, and it is the most valuable thing in this file

**Our histogram kernel is not slow. It is roughly TWICE AS FAST per update as
CatBoost's** (~2.7 cycles/update against ~5.3). The entire symmetric 4x is
SCALING, and the mechanism is BIN-MATRIX READ DENSITY.

We keep a per-leaf `List[Int]` of row ids, so the gather into
`bins[f * n_rows + r]` gets sparser as the tree deepens: about 1 useful byte
in 64 by depth 5. Roughly **3.9 GB of line traffic per tree against CatBoost's
480 MB**. CatBoost compacts the fold once per LEVEL so density stays at ~1/2
and the prefetcher streams it.

**Corrections to things we believed:**
- CatBoost is FEATURE-PARALLEL at our scale too. Their document-range
  parallelism needs `DevScoreCalcObjBlockSize = 5,000,000` rows to engage.
  "They parallelize differently in kind" is dead.
- **DO NOT RETRY THE ROW-MAJOR LAYOUT.** Built, measured 1.15-1.35x SLOWER.
  Layout fixes line utilization but forces every feature's histogram slice
  resident; compaction fixes density without enlarging the scatter set.
- The footprint-by-address model is DEAD. Compact histograms measured 1.006x
  across three runs. Do not quote the 22.3 ms / 38 percent prize again.

## THE LESSON, and it cost four defects in one day

**I shipped four changes that did not execute**, and every one passed a
prediction-digest check: the compact histogram switch, the row pool twice
(leaf-wise, then symmetric), and the `made`/`served` counters installed

A digest cannot distinguish a working change from a no-op. **Verifying the
OUTPUT is unchanged is not verifying the CODE RAN.** For anything whose
correct behavior is "same answer, less work", the only proof is to sabotage
it, watch the digest move, and restore. Do that before committing, every time.

Second lesson: **arithmetic over a profile predicted 38 percent and delivered
1**, three times. The phase profile says the accumulate is 62 percent of the
round; three independent rearrangements of it moved nothing. Measure the
change, not the model of the change.

## GPU cost attribution, 2026-08-19 (static, INFERRED not measured)

Covtype GPU 46.11 s, decomposed by counting command buffers off the source
and pricing them with the repo's own recorded unit costs. Read the caveat at
the bottom before spending a day on any line of it.

**The shipped path**, all at defaults (the run's env capture records only
`MODULAR_HOME`): leaf-wise, `num_leaves=256`, `max_depth=8`, split strategy
AUTO resolving to DEVICE, grower `grow_tree_device_resident`, gradients on
device, multiclass as **7 independent trees per round, sequential** (the
batched class kernel needs `MOJOTREES_GPU_CLASS_BATCH`), launch fusions and
K=1 speculation OFF, partition-tail fusion ON, **GPU row compaction OFF**.

**Launch census, verified against the closed form in
`gpu_resident_round.mojo:1730`**: `8 + 9 * (num_leaves - 1)` = 2,303 command
buffers per tree, plus the iota seed. At 100 rounds x 7 classes = 700 trees,
that is **1.61 million command buffers per fit**, plus 2,200 objective
kernels. Explicit synchronizes: 1,400. No per-split waits on this plane.

**Two terms carry the whole clock:**

| term | modeled | share |
|---|---|---|
| histogram gather DRAM traffic | 28.5 s | 62% |
| command-buffer floor at 14.58 us | 20.9 s | 45% |
| everything else (every sync, every readback, every transfer, the entire multiclass objective) | 1.3 s | under 3% |

Model total 50.7 s against a measured 46.11 s, so it over-attributes by 10
percent and there is no unexplained residual.

**The single largest line is depths 6, 7 and 8: 18.9 s, 41 percent, in 224 of
the 2,304 buffers per tree.** Same mechanism as the CPU diagnosis: bins are
feature-major at `bins[f * n_rows + r]`, an uncompacted node at depth `d`
reads with stride `2^d`, and once that exceeds a cache line the node costs a
FULL `n_rows * nf` pass however few rows it owns. `max_depth=8` puts the last
three levels in that regime. Every previous GPU crossover measurement was at
`num_leaves=31`, depth ~5, which stops one level short of saturation. That is
why this shape is worse than anything we had measured.

The 20.9 s floor term is caused entirely by `num_leaves=256`: 2,048 of the
2,304 buffers per tree do single-digit-microsecond work and are billed at
14.58 us.

**Synchronization is definitively not the answer**, 0.85 s at the outside.
Corroborated by the only host instrument that has run on this plane
(`RESUME_2026-08-18.md:29-38`): `device_wait` 0 calls, 0 ns.

### The one unmeasured number the whole table turns on

The GPU cache line `L`. At `L=64` the histogram term is 28.5 s; at `L=128` it
is **40.0 s** and the model overshoots by 33 percent instead of 10. The repo
names `L` unknown and names the experiment that would settle it
(`gpu_active_rows.mojo:2455-2476`). Measure `L` before trusting the split
between the two terms.

### Caveat, and it is the standing one

This is arithmetic over a profile. Arithmetic over a profile predicted 38

records a fourth: removing 21.5 percent of the launches moved the clock by
1.004x, so the per-buffer floor does not compose additively. Treat the table
as a ranking of where to point an experiment, not as a prediction of a win.
